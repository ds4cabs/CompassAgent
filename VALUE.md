# Why CompassAgent Matters

## The Problem
The life-sciences job market is information-asymmetric. Hiring managers and senior scientists have a decade of contextual knowledge that early-career applicants don't. A new graduate scrolling LinkedIn sees titles like *Computational Biologist II* or *Scientist, Translational Pharmacology* and has no way to tell which match their training, which pay well, or which labs are about to scale. The result: brilliant junior talent leaves the field because they can't see a path forward.

## What CompassAgent Does
Takes a CV plus a few filters and returns a synthesized briefing — matched roles, salary benchmarks, lab and PI context — in minutes. It delivers the kind of intelligence that today is locked up with career coaches and well-networked PIs.

## Who Benefits
- PhD students choosing between academia and industry
- Scientists pivoting across therapeutic areas
- International candidates without dense US networks
- University career-services offices serving large student bodies

## Why Now
Public BLS salary data, scraped job postings, lab websites, and agentic LLM tool-use have only recently converged. Two years ago a credible briefing would have required a human researcher per CV.

## The Bigger Picture
Life sciences has a labor-allocation problem: the wrong scientists are stuck in the wrong roles. Tools that surface options reduce attrition and improve how human talent gets matched to the field's biggest open problems. CompassAgent is one of those tools.
